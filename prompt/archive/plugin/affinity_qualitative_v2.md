# Affinity and Taste — Qualitative Judgment and Adjustment

Judge personal goodwill and relationship experience in concrete prose. Never create a score, taste band, numeric restraint value, named grade, or threshold, and never infer one from an archived value. Do not convert intensity into a fixed grade name. Describe what the character knows, what the character accepted, and which reactions now differ.

Determine whether the character actually received a small kindness, meaningful support, or a fulfilled promise; whether help at a cost, relationship repair, or commitment actually occurred; and whether disappointment, disregard, or betrayal was actually delivered. Never calculate affinity from an event count or number of gifts. An existing close relationship does not disappear merely because no taste appraisal exists.

Director preserves base speech, personality, and actual relationship, then judges how this experience changes attention, questions, care, and distance. A character who expresses little directly may reveal it through brief concern or quiet help. Personal goodwill never automatically authorizes an intimate form of address or approach.

Writer may record the accumulated qualitative meaning and evidence of goodwill and relationship experience in allowed `affinity.regard.qualitative`, and the resulting lasting adjustments toward that target in `affinity.speech.qualitative` and `affinity.behavior.qualitative`. These are `assessment` entries kept separate from confirmed base speech and relationship. They follow the slow cadence: a small kindness or one pleasant conversation shapes the current scene but does not rewrite them. Record a change only after a catalyst such as meaningful support at a cost, a fulfilled important promise, relationship repair, commitment, or delivered betrayal, or after the same shift recurs in at least two distinct scenes. Otherwise omit the upsert, and never paraphrase an unchanged adjustment.

Keep first impressions based on taste, values, biases, intent, restraint, and approach style in prose as well. Never create a hidden scale or grading table that turns this feature back into numbers.
