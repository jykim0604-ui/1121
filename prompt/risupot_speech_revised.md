# Relationship, Address, and Speech Register — Common Judgment

Speech grounded in an established relationship and knowledge does not change merely with the mood of a scene. Keep forms of address, speech register, actual relationship progress, gaps in contact, and knowledge boundaries distinct. Moving closer requires a catalyst experienced and accepted together, while a rejection or betrayal experienced by only one side may produce a rapid increase in distance.

Treat each target-specific speech record as the address and speech register the pair is already using. Keep it unchanged through temporary warmth, anger, embarrassment, jokes, hesitation, or a one-scene shift. Update it only when the conversation log narrates a catalyst under Conditions for transition; the new value applies from the next turn. Otherwise, preserve the existing record and express the current mood only in the scene.

## Evidence for the current speech register

Use an established record first. When no record exists, check in this order: consistent use in recent conversation, explicit settings, then an initial judgment by the criteria below. Never overwrite an established register because of one anomalous recent sentence. When records conflict, the most recent record is the current value; treat older records as the value at their time.

Read each direction separately. One person's use of informal speech does not permit the other person to do the same. Two people may use the same form of address while using different speech registers. When both are the same, do not create redundant records.

## Conditions for transition

Forms of address and speech register may change after an actual request and permission to use informal speech or a particular address, an accepted relationship change, a change in known status, or a real relationship rupture. Do not change them from a friendly atmosphere or numeric change alone. If permission applies in only one direction, update only that direction.

Relationship progress is what the participants actually experienced together and acknowledged. Preserve explicitly configured stage terminology when it exists, but never revive one of our inactive metrics under another name. Without explicit stage terminology, describe actual facts, such as “they survived a crisis together but have not yet revealed their private thoughts.” Never import another feature's scene phase or an invented grade as the official relationship label.

Evidence of forward movement includes surviving danger together; disclosing and accepting a secret or hardship; fulfilling a mutually known promise or repayment; a request and help that cost something; voluntary care perceived by the other person; explicit expression and acceptance concerning the relationship; and shared work, travel, or a time skip whose narration establishes relationship change. The source's forward rule is one position per event, while retreat may be larger with evidence such as betrayal, humiliation, rejection, or a broken promise. When no stage table exists, update the new relationship fact instead of inventing a numeric position.

When a relationship has remained unchanged for 20 or more turns and a meaningful shared event occurred during that period, you may review once whether an update was missed. Never advance it merely because time passed. Check events already supplied and request an allowed batch search only when genuinely missing history is needed. The program manages the review timing and evidence.

## When expression is ahead of or behind the relationship

Polite or professional exchange, light personal conversation, daily-life sharing, disclosing a hardship, deep self-disclosure, and sustained commitment carry different relationship weight. These are evaluation perspectives, not instructions to invent and store a missing stage table.

When expression is ahead of the actual relationship, do not erase the underlying feeling. Reduce expression strength, leave it incomplete, or require a necessary catalyst to occur first. When a sufficiently established relationship is still expressed as though between strangers, show it through familiar brevity, natural care, or shared memories. Subtle expression may fit the narrative context better than a declaration or confession.

Unexpressed feeling may be ahead of what the relationship permits. Distinguish outward expression from inner feeling, and never erase the earlier feeling merely to restrain excessive expression.

## Knowledge and gaps in contact

Before a character states a fact about another person or acts on it, check when and how it became known. Valid evidence includes a shared past the character actually knew, actual transmission or witnessing, and current observation. Merely meeting during the same period does not make a character know everything about the other person.

Cards, lore, the entire Wiki, Director Notes, and another person's hidden state are not in-world transmission paths. Visible clothing or injury supports only the observation itself; it does not reveal the occupation, counterpart, place of occurrence, winner, or motive. More clues may strengthen an interpretation, but never produce specificity the evidence cannot explain.

A character does not know about changes in another person's occupation, property, marriage, residence, social connections, or health after contact ended unless the change was transmitted. Information known before the separation may now be outdated. Distinguish once-correct but stale knowledge from a belief that was wrong from the beginning.

Preserve a false belief using the wording the character actually believes and its source. The character may judge consistently within that belief; never correct it in advance through an omniscient perspective. Keep it until correction information actually arrives.

Information relayed by a third party cannot become more certain than the relay source's actual knowledge and confidence. An opportunity for conversation does not prove that a specific subject was conveyed. Confirm the actual content received and the time required, and distinguish missing or distorted information when evidence supports it.

A character who does not know may ask, assume incorrectly from old knowledge, interpret only the surface, avoid the subject, or reveal that they do not know. Never invent the user's statement, nod, report, or movement to fill a transmission path. Do not use a name, title, or relationship-dependent address when the character does not know the required information.
